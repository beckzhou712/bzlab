Physics Informed Machine Learning: Resource Index

11 Oct, 2026
|Edit page

The full video series (YouTube playlist). Below, in course order, are the related papers and code, each with a short note.

00 · Overview: AI/ML and physics

Introduces the two main threads, “using known physics to improve learning” and “discovering physics from data,” along with the five entry points: problem, data, architecture, loss, and optimization.

  • Kapteyn et al., A probabilistic graphical model foundation for enabling predictive digital twins at scale: a framework for continually absorbing data from a physical asset and updating its digital twin model.
  • Brunton, Noack, Koumoutsakos, Machine Learning for Fluid Mechanics: a review of machine learning in fluid modeling, reduced-order modeling, and control; good background reading for the course.

01 · Choosing what to model

Distinguishes tasks such as equation discovery, surrogate simulation, error correction, and digital twins; the first thing to ask is what problem the model is meant to solve.

  • Ling, Kurzawski, Templeton, Reynolds averaged turbulence modelling using deep neural networks with embedded invariance: builds invariance into the architecture of a Reynolds-stress closure model; a model case of “known physics + unknown function.”
  • The digital twin paper by Kapteyn et al.: matches this lecture’s picture of a multi-fidelity, continually updated model.

02 · Curating training data

Covers symmetry-based data augmentation, the choice of coordinates, simulation versus experiment, multi-fidelity data, rare events, and weak signals.

  • Champion et al., Data-driven discovery of coordinates and governing equations: learns effective coordinates and dynamics at the same time from high-dimensional observations; discussed in depth in lectures 08 and 11.
  • Authors’ code: SindyAutoencoders: the Lorenz, reaction–diffusion, and pendulum examples from the paper above.

03 · Designing an architecture

The architecture bounds the function space that can be searched; priors such as low dimensionality, sparsity, and symmetry narrow it down.

  • The invariant turbulence network of Ling et al.: puts tensor structure into the network, showing that physics can be guaranteed by the architecture.
  • The SINDy autoencoder of Champion et al.: combines an encoder with a sparse dynamics model.
  • Ronneberger, Fischer, Brox, U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation: the original U-Net paper, used to illustrate a multiscale architectural prior.
  • Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks: simulates complex physical processes with local interaction rules in a graph network.

04 · Crafting a loss function

Equation residuals, conservation, and sparsity regularizers encourage the model to obey physics; a loss penalty is usually a soft constraint.

  • Wang et al., Towards Physics-informed Deep Learning for Turbulent Flow Prediction: TF-Net and its divergence-constraint example.
  • Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis, the original PINN paper: adds the PDE residual to the training objective; expanded on in lecture 23.
  • The SINDy autoencoder of Champion et al.: uses reconstruction, dynamics, and sparsity losses together.

05 · Employing an optimization algorithm

Satisfying a structure exactly through constrained optimization is stronger than only penalizing violations in the loss.

  • Loiseau, Brunton, Constrained Sparse Galerkin Regression: enforces energy-related constraints in sparse reduced-order fluid models.
  • Baddoo et al., Physics-informed dynamic mode decomposition (piDMD): restricts the DMD fit to sets of matrices that satisfy properties such as conservation and symmetry.
  • Otto et al., A Unified Framework to Enforce, Discover, and Promote Symmetry in Machine Learning: a unified framework that organizes enforcing, discovering, and promoting symmetry.

06 · Recap of the first five stages

Reviews the five stages and the goals of being “interpretable, generalizable, and parsimonious.” This lecture mostly revisits the papers above and has no separate new core method.

07 · Preview of upcoming modules

Previews sparse model discovery, PINNs, operator learning, symmetry, digital twins, and benchmark problems; the specific resources are listed under the later lectures.

08 · Deep learning to discover coordinates for dynamics

Uses autoencoders to find coordinates in which the dynamics are low-dimensional, sparse, or approximately linear.

  • Champion et al., Data-driven discovery of coordinates and governing equations · code: discovers sparse equations with SINDy in the latent variables.
  • Lusch, Kutz, Brunton, Deep learning for universal linear embeddings of nonlinear dynamics · code: learns Koopman coordinates that linearize nonlinear dynamics.
  • Brunton et al., Modern Koopman Theory for Dynamical Systems: a long review covering Koopman representations, numerical methods, and applications.
  • PyKoopman: a Python library for computing data-driven approximations of the Koopman operator.

09 · SINDy: discovering sparse equations from data

An overview of the candidate function library, sparse regression, ODE/PDE discovery, and the challenges that data and coordinates bring.

  • Brunton, Proctor, Kutz, Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems: introduces the original SINDy method.
  • Rudy et al., Data-driven discovery of partial differential equations: extends sparse equation discovery to PDEs (PDE-FIND).
  • PySINDy · software paper: a Python package implementing feature libraries, derivative estimation, and a range of sparse optimizers.

10 · SINDy: training data and disambiguating models

Noise, derivative estimation, and sampling coverage determine whether candidate equations can be told apart; the weak form uses integration to reduce sensitivity to noise.

  • Schaeffer, McCalla, Sparse model selection via integral terms: identifies dynamics through integral terms, reducing the noise sensitivity of direct differentiation.
  • Gurevich et al., Using noisy or incomplete data to discover models of spatiotemporal dynamics: the weak form applied to noisy or incomplete flow-field observations.
  • Alves, Fiuza, Data-driven discovery of reduced plasma physics models from fully kinetic simulations: discovers reduced PDE models from kinetic plasma simulations.

11 · SINDy: effective coordinates for parsimonious models

The wrong coordinates make a simple equation dense; autoencoders and delay embedding can help with hidden variables.

  • Joint discovery of coordinates and equations by Champion et al.: a nonlinear autoencoder finds latent coordinates suited to sparse dynamics.
  • Brunton et al., Chaos as an Intermittently Forced Linear System: the HAVOK method combines delay coordinates, Koopman ideas, and sparse regression.
  • The constrained fluid models of Loiseau and Brunton: a related example of adding physical constraints to reduced-order fluid modeling.

12 · SINDy: the library of candidate nonlinearities

Candidate terms are designed from the variables, control inputs, parameters, and physical symmetries; a library that is too large, or columns that are highly correlated, make identification difficult.

  • Mangan et al., Inferring biological networks by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics: implicit-SINDy recasts rational-function dynamics as implicit sparse identification.
  • Brunton, Proctor, Kutz, Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics with Control (SINDYc): adds external forcing and control inputs to the candidate function library.
  • Kaheman et al., SINDy-PI: A Robust Algorithm for Parallel Implicit Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics · code: parallel implicit identification, improving on the original method’s sensitivity to noise.
  • Gelß et al., Multidimensional approximation of nonlinear dynamical systems: MANDy uses a tensor-train representation to ease the computational burden of high-dimensional candidate libraries.
  • Tropp, Greed is Good: Algorithmic Results for Sparse Approximation: theoretical background on sparse recovery conditions; the paper was published in 2004 (the year mentioned in the course is a rough recollection).

13 · SINDy: the optimization algorithms

Compares sequential threshold least squares, SR3, constrained sparse regression, and stability constraints; models are selected by trading error against complexity.

  • Zheng et al., A Unified Framework for Sparse Relaxed Regularized Regression: SR3: a relaxation variable makes sparse regression more flexible.
  • Mangan et al., Model selection for dynamical systems via sparse regression and information criteria: screens the Pareto candidate models further with information criteria.
  • Kaptanoglu et al., Promoting global stability in data-driven models of quadratic nonlinear dynamics: promotes global stability when identifying quadratic dynamics.
  • Quade et al., Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics for Rapid Model Recovery · authors’ code: quickly updates an existing sparse model after an abrupt change in the system.
  • PySINDy: includes ready-to-run optimizers such as STLSQ and SR3.

14 · Discrepancy modeling

Keep the trusted first-principles model and focus machine learning on the unmodeled physics and the gap between simulation and experiment. This lecture is mainly about the modeling approach and does not introduce a separately named algorithm or codebase; for a related turbulence-closure example, see the Ling et al. paper under lecture 01.

15 · Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNN)

The network learns a Hamiltonian, and its partial derivatives give the evolution of the state, which brings mechanical structure into the model.

  • Greydanus, Dzamba, Yosinski, Hamiltonian Neural Networks · authors’ code: learns energy-related dynamical structure from observations.

16 · Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNN)

The network learns a Lagrangian and builds the Euler–Lagrange dynamics through automatic differentiation; suited to cases where conjugate momenta are hard to obtain.

  • Cranmer et al., Lagrangian Neural Networks · authors’ code: includes double-pendulum and relativistic-particle examples.
  • Matthew West, Variational Integrators (2004 PhD thesis): background on variational integrators.

17 · Neural Implicit Flow (NIF)

Represents parameterized spatiotemporal fields with a shape net and a parameter net, supporting dimensionality reduction and reconstruction on irregular meshes.

  • Pan, Brunton, Kutz, Neural Implicit Flow: a mesh-agnostic dimensionality reduction paradigm of spatio-temporal data: the original NIF paper.
  • Authors’ code · documentation and tutorials: a runnable implementation and examples.

18 · Neural ODEs (NODEs)

Learns the continuous-time vector field directly and advances the state with a numerical integrator; can handle irregular sampling.

  • Chen et al., Neural Ordinary Differential Equations: the original paper on Neural ODEs and the adjoint sensitivity method.
  • torchdiffeq: the authors’ differentiable ODE solvers for PyTorch, with examples.
  • The ResNet paper by He et al.: for the relation between discrete residual updates and the continuous Neural ODE, see lecture 20.

19 · Python Symbolic Regression (PySR)

Uses evolutionary search and constant optimization to find readable formulas that balance error and complexity.

  • Cranmer, Interpretable Machine Learning for Science with PySR and SymbolicRegression.jl: PySR’s method, implementation, and benchmarks.
  • PySR (Python) · SymbolicRegression.jl (Julia backend): the two projects you actually run.
  • Bongard, Lipson, Automated reverse engineering of nonlinear dynamical systems; Schmidt, Lipson, Distilling Free-Form Natural Laws from Experimental Data: early symbolic-discovery work mentioned in the course.

20 · Residual Networks (ResNet)

A residual block learns the difference between input and output; applied to dynamical systems, it can be read as an Euler-style update in discrete time.

  • He et al., Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition: introduces deep residual networks and skip connections.
  • The Neural ODE paper by Chen et al.: takes the residual update further, to continuous time.

21 · Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)

Builds operator layers in Fourier space to learn the map from an input function to the PDE’s solution function, and discusses generalization across resolutions and its limits.

  • Li et al., Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations: the original FNO paper, with the zero-shot super-resolution experiments.
  • Kovachki et al., Neural Operator: Learning Maps Between Function Spaces With Applications to PDEs: the more general neural operator framework.
  • Laplace neural operator for solving differential equations: another transform-domain operator architecture.
  • NeuralOperator / neuralop: an open-source Python library that includes FNO, with tutorials.

22 · Deep Operator Networks (DeepONet)

A branch net encodes the input function, a trunk net encodes the output location, and the two are combined to predict the solution operator.

  • Lu et al., Learning nonlinear operators via DeepONet based on the universal approximation theorem of operators: the original DeepONet paper.
  • Authors’ code · DeepXDE: the implementation from the original paper, and a software library that supports DeepONet and PINNs.

23 · Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Jointly optimizes the data error and the PDE residual over observation points and virtual collocation points; well suited to filling in a physical field from sparse observations, though training can be difficult.

  • Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis, Physics-informed neural networks · original code: the classic PINN paper and implementation; the original repository is marked as no longer actively maintained.
  • Raissi et al., Hidden fluid mechanics · code: infers velocity and pressure fields from flow-visualization data.
  • Pang, Lu, Karniadakis, fPINNs; Δ-PINNs: handle fractional-order operators and complex geometries, respectively.
  • Krishnapriyan et al., Characterizing possible failure modes in physics-informed neural networks; Rohrhofer et al., Data vs. Physics: The Apparent Pareto Front of Physics-Informed Neural Networks: explain training failures, curriculum regularization, and the trade-off between the data loss and the physics loss.
  • Ben Moseley’s PINN introduction and oscillator code; Juan Diego Toscano’s PINN/DeepONet tutorial code; DeepXDE: good material for following along hands-on.
